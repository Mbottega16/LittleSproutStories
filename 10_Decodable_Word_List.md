# Decodable Word List — Stream B

Written August 2026 (Roadmap 0.1); updated October 2026 to incorporate the **80/20 Decodability & Engaging Story Flow Framework** (see [GUIDANCE_DECODABILITY_VS_STORY_FLOW.md](GUIDANCE_DECODABILITY_VS_STORY_FLOW.md) for full pedagogical rationale and rubric).

## The problem this fixes

A screen of the original 112 Stream B lines (8 per book × 14 books) found 118 of 198 unique words falling outside basic CVC patterns plus a kindergarten sight-word list. Lines like *"Sophie feels embarrassed"* and *"Marcus understands"* are simplified English, not decodable text — a 5-year-old at the decoding stage cannot sound them out. 

Stream A (read by a grown-up or neural TTS) retains full literary complexity; Stream B (read *by the child*) is held to the balanced rules below.

## The 80/20 Decodability Target

Stream B balances phonics precision with narrative charm. Each line targets **80% to 85% decodable vocabulary** (matching the child's developmental tier) with the remaining **15% to 20% reserved for high-frequency sight words, connectors, and 1 picture-grounded theme noun**.

## Approved categories

A word in Stream B must belong to one of these approved categories:

**(a) CVC / CVCC short-vowel words**, including common initial or final consonant blends (`bl, br, cl, cr, dr, fl, fr, gl, gr, pl, pr, sc, sk, sl, sm, sn, sp, st, sw, tr`) and digraphs (`ck, sh, ch, th, ng, wh`) — e.g. *big, hop, glad, truck, still, sand, bell, chest, quit, fast, spot, drop*.

**(b) Dolch pre-primer & primer sight words** — the high-frequency grammatical mortar: `a, and, are, at, away, be, big, blue, but, came, can, come, did, do, down, each, eat, find, for, four, funny, get, go, going, good, had, has, have, he, help, her, here, him, his, how, I, in, into, is, it, jump, know, like, little, look, made, make, me, my, new, no, not, now, of, on, one, our, out, play, please, ran, red, run, said, saw, see, she, so, some, soon, that, the, their, them, then, there, they, think, this, three, to, too, two, under, up, want, was, we, well, went, were, what, where, will, with, yes, you`.

**(c) Character names** — free pass (`Maya, Marcus, Sophie, James`). Children learn these as recognizable logos.

**(d) One concrete, picture-supported theme noun per line** — a word tied directly to what the illustration and backdrop already show (`doctor, robot, garden, letter, timer, mail, flour, school, town, room, truck, ears, rabbit`). The visual carries the meaning, preventing invented filler words.

**(e) Number words** — `one, two, three, four`.

**(f) Multi-syllable words built entirely from simple CVC syllables** — `visit, letter, muffin, rabbit, sunset, upset` decode one simple syllable at a time.

**(g) Story Heart Words (Pre-Taught, Max 1–2 per book)** — partially irregular or advanced high-frequency words essential to the narrative or emotional beat (e.g. `brave, calm, proud, care, share, love, try, friend, safe`). Heart Words are introduced on the cover/intro screen for grown-up pre-teaching so the child never has to guess.

## The Expanded Feelings & SEL Set

*Important update (October 2026):* The original restriction to only six words (`glad, sad, mad, bad, OK, good`) flattened emotional nuance and caused harmful mislabeling (e.g., labeling Marcus's siren anxiety as "feeling bad", or James's schedule frustration as "mad").

### Banned Phrase: "Feels Bad" for Anxiety or Shyness
Children should **never** read *"feels bad"* when experiencing anxiety, shyness, doubt, or sensory overload. That phrasing connotes moral wrongdoing. Instead, use:
1. **Accurate Decodable Feeling Words:** `shy, calm, safe, upset, stuck, unsure, lost`.
2. **Designated Story Heart Words:** `brave, proud, afraid`.
3. **Behavioral / Somatic Actions:** Describe what the character actually does decodably:
   - *"The bell is loud. Marcus covers his ears."* (replaces *"Marcus feels bad"*)
   - *"James stops. 'My list is stuck,' said James."* (replaces *"James feels mad"*)
   - *"Sophie looks at the seeds. Will they grow?"* (replaces *"Sophie is sad"*)

### Tiered SEL Vocabulary by Age

- **Tier 1 (Ages 4–5 / Early K, Books 1–5):** `glad, sad, mad, calm, safe, upset, stuck, lost, OK, good`, plus Heart Words `brave, kind, warm`.
- **Tier 2 (Ages 5–6 / Late K – Early G1, Books 6–12):** adds `proud, brave, afraid, shy, quiet, cross, bold, fine, warm`.
- **Tier 3 (Ages 6–7 / G1 – Early G2, Books 14–19+):** adds `nervous, excited, hopeful, grumpy, worried, patient, gentle, caring`.

## The "Zero Invented Props" Rule

Authors must **never invent random CVC objects** (e.g., giving a firefighter a *"bag"* or a police officer a *"pin"*) solely to satisfy phonics rules. If a decodable noun is unavailable, write the line using character action, dialogue, or an approved picture-supported theme noun.

## What's explicitly still banned

- Multi-syllable abstract Latinate words without picture support (`embarrassed, impatient, understands, remembers, disagree, different, favorite`).
- Unintroduced silent-e or complex vowel teams in Tier 1 (Books 1–5) unless designated as the book's 1 pre-taught Story Heart Word.
- Any feeling word that equates normal emotional vulnerability with being "bad."

## Acceptance & Quality Rubric

Every Stream B line must pass the **5-Point Golden Sprout Screen**:
1. Decodability ratio $\ge 80\%$
2. Emotional truth (no false "bad" / "mad")
3. Zero invented CVC props (must match scene art)
4. Natural prosody and cadence when spoken aloud
5. Double-Engine synergy with Stream A and the mid-page quiz
