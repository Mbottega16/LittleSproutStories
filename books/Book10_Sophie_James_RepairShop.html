Sophie & James
Visit the Repair Shop

🤖
James’s favorite toy robot had a broken arm, and today David the repair helper would try to fix it.
James’s robot is broken.
👨‍🔧
David
“Will it be exactly like before?” James asked, worried.
James is worried.
👨‍🔧
David
David looked closely at the robot. “I can fix it, but you might still see the repair line,” he said honestly.
James feels sad.

How does James feel?

Sophie squeezed his hand. “Sometimes fixed things get a new kind of special,” she said.
Fixed things are special.
👨‍🔧
David
🔧
David carefully glued and reinforced the little arm while James watched every step.
David fixes the arm.
🤖
When it was done, a thin seam ran across the robot’s shoulder — but the arm moved just fine.
The seam shows.
James looked at the seam for a long moment, then grinned. “That’s his battle scar now,” he said proudly.
James feels proud.

How does James feel about the seam by the end?

👨‍🔧
David
Sophie and James thanked David and promised to bring anything else that broke straight to him.
They thank David.

For Grown-Ups

Feeling/skill: Small grief eases when a repaired thing is seen as changed, not ruined.

Ask your child: Has something you loved ever broken and gotten fixed?

Try this: Fix something small together and talk about its new marks.

Standards: RI.K.8, L.K.4.a
